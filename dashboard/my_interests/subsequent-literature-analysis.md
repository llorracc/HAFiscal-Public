## What the subsequent literature tells us

The citing papers cluster around three main directions. **Monetary policy and inequality** is prominent: Lenza, Slačálek, and coauthors (2019–2024) study how QE and conventional monetary policy affect income and wealth inequality in the Euro area, using household-level data and HANK-style frameworks to trace heterogeneity in MPCs and portfolio exposure. This builds directly on insights that monetary transmission depends on the wealth distribution and heterogeneous consumption responses. **Saving behavior and wealth dynamics** is another strand: Fagereng, Holm, Moll, and Natvik (2019) show that capital gains matter for saving behavior across the wealth distribution, and Ma, Stachurski, and Toda (2019) extend theory on the income fluctuation problem to study the evolution of wealth. **Fiscal and structural policy** is the third theme: Guner, Lopez-Segovia, and Ramos (2021) analyze individual income tax reform in Spain, Boar and Midrigin (2019) study markups and inequality, and Nakajima and Ríos-Rull (2014) connect credit and bankruptcy to aggregate fluctuations. Methodologically, work on dynamic programming with state-dependent discounting (Stachurski and Zhang 2019) and on unbounded-reward choice (Ma and Stachurski 2019) extends the toolbox for heterogeneous-agent models. Cutting-edge areas include the combination of monetary policy, inequality, and household balance sheets, as well as the role of capital gains and heterogeneity in returns for wealth dynamics.

## Most important subsequent papers

1. **Lenza and Slačálek (2019–2024)** – *"How Does Monetary Policy Affect Income and Wealth Inequality? Evidence from Quantitative Easing in the Euro Area"* – Central empirical work on monetary policy and inequality; applies HANK-style logic to Euro area QE and motivates a large empirical literature.
2. **Fagereng, Holm, Moll, and Natvik (2019)** – *"Saving Behavior Across the Wealth Distribution: The Importance of Capital Gains"* – Shows that capital gains drive saving behavior at the top; directly relevant for models of wealth heterogeneity and fiscal/monetary transmission.
3. **Ma, Stachurski, and Toda (2019)** – *"The income fluctuation problem and the evolution of wealth"* – Theoretical advances linking the income fluctuation problem to wealth dynamics; extends the toolkit for HA models.
4. **Nakajima and Ríos-Rull (2014)** – *"Credit, Bankruptcy, and Aggregate Fluctuations"* – Connects credit frictions and bankruptcy to aggregate dynamics; foundational for heterogeneity–business-cycle work.
5. **Guner, Lopez-Segovia, and Ramos (2021)** – *"Reforming the Individual Income Tax in Spain"* – Applied analysis of tax reform with heterogeneous agents; exemplifies how HA frameworks are used for policy evaluation.
